<h2><strong><span style="font-size:17pt;font-family:Arial,sans-serif;">Plan the Layout Before You Touch Anything</span></strong></h2>
<h3><strong><span style="font-size:13pt;font-family:Arial,sans-serif;">Check the Work Triangle and Walkways</span></strong></h3>
<p><span style="font-size:11pt;font-family:Arial,sans-serif;">Most efficient kitchens connect the sink, cooktop and fridge in a loose triangle, with each leg roughly between 1.2 and 2.7 meters (4 to 9 feet). Keep main aisles about 100 cm (40 inches) wide, or around 120 cm (48 inches) if two people cook together.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-size:11pt;font-family:Arial,sans-serif;">If you cannot change the layout, adjust what sits inside it. Move the trash and recycling closer to the prep area, and keep the most-used pots near the stove.</span></p>

<p><span style="font-size:11pt;font-family:Arial,sans-serif;">Every appliance needs a clear counter beside it so hot pans and heavy bags have somewhere to go. A common guideline is at least 40 cm (15 inches) next to the cooktop and fridge.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-size:11pt;font-family:Arial,sans-serif;">Clearing these zones does more for daily comfort than most decorative changes.</span></p>

<h2><strong><span style="font-size:17pt;font-family:Arial,sans-serif;">Ventilation, Hygiene and Cleaning Routines</span></strong></h2>
<h3><strong><span style="font-size:13pt;font-family:Arial,sans-serif;">Ventilation First</span></strong></h3>
<p><span style="font-size:11pt;font-family:Arial,sans-serif;">A range hood that vents outdoors removes grease, moisture and cooking fumes, which keeps walls and cabinets cleaner. Recirculating hoods help less, so clean or replace their filters on schedule.</span></p>
<h3><strong><span style="font-size:13pt;font-family:Arial,sans-serif;">Easy Cleaning Habits</span></strong></h3>
<p><span style="font-size:11pt;font-family:Arial,sans-serif;">Wash dishes after cooking, wipe the cooktop while it is still warm, and empty the bin before it smells. Check the fridge weekly and discard expired food, keeping it at or below 4&deg;C (40&deg;F) and the freezer at -18&deg;C (0&deg;F).</span></p>
<h2><strong><span style="font-size:17pt;font-family:Arial,sans-serif;">Flooring, Faucets and Appliances</span></strong></h2>
<p><span style="font-size:11pt;font-family:Arial,sans-serif;">Choose flooring that handles spills and dropped items, such as porcelain tile or good-quality luxury vinyl plank, and add anti-fatigue mats where you stand to cook. Avoid glossy tiles in wet zones unless they have a slip-resistant rating.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-size:11pt;font-family:Arial,sans-serif;">A pull-down faucet makes sink cleaning easier, and a quiet, efficient dishwasher saves time and water. When replacing appliances, check dimensions carefully and look at energy labels before buying.</span></p>
<h2><strong><span style="font-size:17pt;font-family:Arial,sans-serif;">Safety Rules You Should Not Skip</span></strong></h2>
<p><span style="font-size:11pt;font-family:Arial,sans-serif;">Outlets near sinks should be protected with ground-fault circuit interrupters where local rules require them. Hire a licensed electrician for new circuits, panel work and hardwired appliances.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-size:11pt;font-family:Arial,sans-serif;">Gas appliances and gas lines must be handled by a qualified professional, and every kitchen needs a working smoke alarm and a small fire extinguisher within reach. Never hide a leak, damp or mold problem behind new finishes.</span></p>
